# The Fake Internship That Built a Real Product

It was a late Tuesday night, semester exams just over, when I found it — a sleek
internship listing for an "AI Research Intern" role at what looked like a promising
startup. The description was perfect. Cutting-edge NLP work, a stipend, a brand name
I could put on my resume. I spent two hours polishing my application.

I never heard back.

But that wasn't the worst part. Digging deeper, I realized the company's LinkedIn had
150 employees but zero mutual connections. The domain was registered three weeks ago.
The "team" photos were stock images. The listing had been copy-pasted across seven
different job boards with slightly different company names each time.

I had almost fallen for it. And I knew hundreds of other students — less careful ones,
more desperate ones — probably did fall for it every single day.

That night, instead of sleeping, I started building.

Prism is a job posting credibility engine — a Chrome extension that analyzes internship
and job listings in real time, cross-references company signals, and flags suspicious
patterns before you waste your hope on something fake.

It started as frustration. It became a product.

I'm Ganga, a third-year B.Tech AI student who believes the best things you build come
from your own worst experiences. Still building. Still learning.
